What Are Fractions?

A fraction represents a part of a whole. The word "whole" means a complete unit or object that can be divided into equal parts. For example, a whole pizza, a whole hour, or a whole dollar.

A fraction consists of two numbers separated by a line:

3
4
↑ NUMERATOR
(parts you have)
↓ DENOMINATOR
(total equal parts)
  • Numerator (top number): Tells you how many parts you have
  • Denominator (bottom number): Tells you how many equal parts make up one whole

Example: In the fraction 3/4, you have 3 parts out of 4 equal parts that make up the whole.

How to Simplify Fractions (Step-by-Step)

Simplifying a fraction means writing it in its simplest form by dividing both the numerator and denominator by their common factors (numbers that divide evenly into both).

Example: Simplify 15/60

Method 1: Factor by factor
15/60 = ?
• Both 15 and 60 are divisible by 5
• 15 ÷ 5 = 3
• 60 ÷ 5 = 12
• So 15/60 = 3/12

• Both 3 and 12 are divisible by 3
• 3 ÷ 3 = 1
• 12 ÷ 3 = 4
• So 3/12 = 1/4

Answer: 15/60 = 1/4
Method 2: Find the Greatest Common Factor (GCF)
• Factors of 15: 1, 3, 5, 15
• Factors of 60: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60
• Common factors: 1, 3, 5, 15
• Greatest Common Factor (GCF) = 15

• 15 ÷ 15 = 1
• 60 ÷ 15 = 4
Answer: 15/60 = 1/4

Why both methods work: We divided by 5 then by 3, which is the same as dividing by 15 (since 5 × 3 = 15)!

Fractions in Everyday Life

🍕 For 9-Year-Olds: Pizza Time!

Imagine you and your friend order a pizza cut into 8 equal slices.

You: 3/8
Friend: 2/8

Who ate more? You did! 3/8 > 2/8. Understanding fractions helps you understand fair sharing and portion sizes.

⏰ Time Management

An hour has 60 minutes. Let's see how fractions help us understand time:

Homework
1/4 hour

1/2 hour
Reading
1/4 hour
  • Homework: 15 min = 15/60 = 1/4 hour
  • Video games: 30 min = 30/60 = 1/2 hour
  • Reading: 15 min = 15/60 = 1/4 hour

Real-world use: Planning your day, understanding that you spent twice as long gaming (1/2 hour) than on homework (1/4 hour).

🍳 Cooking & Recipes

Recipes use fractions constantly! Understanding fractions is essential for adjusting recipe sizes.

Original recipe (serves 4):

  • 3/4 cup flour
  • 1/2 cup sugar

What if you want to make half the recipe (serves 2)?

Multiply each ingredient by 1/2: Flour becomes 3/8 cup, Sugar becomes 1/4 cup, Butter becomes 1/6 cup

💵 Money and Shopping

Understanding fractions helps you calculate discounts and compare prices!

50% OFF
= 1/2 off
25% OFF
= 1/4 off

Example: A $20 toy is 50% off (1/2 off). You pay 1/2 of $20 = $10!

A $12 book is 25% off (1/4 off). You save 1/4 of $12 = $3, so you pay $9.

📏 Sports and Measurements

Athletes and coaches use fractions all the time to measure performance and distances.

3/8 mile
track distance

Example: A running track might be 3/8 of a mile. If you run 3 laps, you've run 3 × 3/8 = 9/8 = 1 1/8 miles!

Basketball: Free throw percentage of 3/4 means making 3 out of every 4 shots (75% success rate).

Why Fractions Matter

Fractions are everywhere in our daily lives! Here's why learning fractions is so important:

🍰 Sharing and Fairness

Fractions help us divide things equally among people. Whether it's splitting a pizza, sharing candy, or dividing chores, fractions ensure everyone gets their fair share.

👨‍🍳 Cooking and Baking

Nearly every recipe uses fractions! Measuring cups come in 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, and 1 cup sizes. Professional chefs use fractions constantly to scale recipes and maintain perfect proportions.

💰 Money and Shopping

Sales and discounts use fractions: "50% off" means paying 1/2 the original price. Understanding fractions helps you calculate the best deals.

⏱️ Time Management

We use fractions to describe time constantly: "Meet me in half an hour" (1/2 hour = 30 minutes). Understanding these helps you plan your schedule effectively.

⚽ Sports and Statistics

Baseball batting averages, basketball shooting percentages, and football completion rates are all fractions! A batting average of .333 means the player gets a hit 1/3 of the time.

Practice makes perfect! The more you work with fractions, the easier they become.
